# Interview Execution Guide - Recipe 1.1
## Problem Validation for 10Demo

---

## Overview

### Venture Context

**Problem Statement:**
B2B SaaS prospects wait 24-120 hours for product demos, experience generic one-size-fits-all presentations, and sales teams are overwhelmed with demo requests—causing 40-60% demo no-shows and massive pipeline leakage.

**Solution Statement:**
10Demo provides instant, 24/7 AI-led personalized product demos with multilingual support and CRM integration—delivering context-aware conversations that convert leads without adding sales headcount.

### Interview Goals

This problem validation research aims to achieve the following KPI targets from Recipe 1.1:

- **I1.1 Pain Score**: ≥7/10 average across interviews
- **I1.2 Workaround Rate**: ≥80% have active workarounds (proof of urgency)
- **I1.3 Recency**: ≥70% experienced problem in last 30 days
- **I1.4 Solution Resonance**: ≥70% express strong interest
- **I1.5 Competitive Displacement**: ≥60% would consider switching
- **I1.6 Outcome Alignment**: ≥80% align with desired outcomes
- **I1.7 Budget Existence**: ≥60% have allocated budget
- **I1.8 Current Spend**: Average ≥$1,000/month on alternatives
- **I1.9 Willingness to Pay**: Acceptable price ≥ 2x cost base
- **I1.10 Purchase Intent**: ≥50% score 4-5/5

**Success Criteria:**
- Complete 10-15 interviews across 3-4 persona types
- Achieve ≥7/10 KPIs meeting targets
- Identify clear patterns in pain points and willingness to pay

- Size: 50-500 employees

## Interview Script

### Pre-Interview Setup

**Before the call:**
- [ ] Review interviewee's LinkedIn and company website
- [ ] Prepare 2-3 personalized questions based on their role
- [ ] Test Zoom/recording (ask permission to record)
- [ ] Have tracking template open for live note-taking
- [ ] Set timer for section pacing (20 min per section)

**Opening (2 minutes):**
"Thanks for joining me today! I'm doing research on how B2B sales teams handle product demos—not pitching anything, just learning. I'll ask about your experience with demo scheduling, delivery, and conversion. There are no right or wrong answers—I'm just trying to understand your world.

This should take about 30 minutes. Mind if I record this for my notes? Everything you share will be anonymized.

Sound good? Let's start with understanding your current setup..."

---

### Section 1: Problem Discovery (20 minutes)
**Goal:** Validate problem existence, severity, and urgency

---

#### Q1: Tell me about your current demo process—walk me through what happens when a lead requests a demo.

**Purpose:** Understand current state and identify friction points (feeds I1.1 Pain Score)

**Listen for:**
- Steps in their process (manual vs automated)
- Handoffs between teams (SDR → AE → Solutions Engineer)
- Time delays and bottlenecks
- Lead experience from their perspective

**Probing Questions:**
- "What happens after someone fills out a demo request form?"
- "How long does it typically take from request to scheduled demo?"
- "Who's involved in scheduling and preparing for demos?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ "It works fine" (no pain = no problem)
- ⚠️ "We don't really do demos" (wrong ICP)
- ⚠️ "Prospects don't mind waiting" (low urgency)

---

#### Q2: When was the last time you personally experienced frustration with your demo process?

**Purpose:** Assess problem recency and personal impact (feeds I1.3 Recency)

**Listen for:**
- Specific date/timeframe (last week vs 6 months ago)
- Emotional language ("annoying," "frustrating," "painful")
- Concrete example with details

**Probing Questions:**
- "What specifically went wrong?"
- "How did that impact you or your team?"
- "How often does this type of thing happen?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ "I can't really remember" (not a frequent pain)
- ⚠️ "Maybe a few months ago?" (not urgent)
- ⚠️ Vague or hypothetical answers

---

#### Q3: On a scale of 0-10, how painful is this demo scheduling and delivery problem for you today?

**Purpose:** Quantify pain intensity (feeds I1.1 Pain Score)

**Listen for:**
- Score 7+ indicates strong pain
- Justification for their score
- Comparison to other business problems

**Probing Questions:**
- "Why did you choose [X] and not higher/lower?"
- "What would need to change for this to become a 10?"
- "What are your top 3 business problems right now—where does this rank?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Score below 6 (insufficient pain)
- ⚠️ "It's annoying but not critical" (low priority)
- ⚠️ Long pause before answering (not top-of-mind)

---

#### Q4: How much time does your team spend on demo-related activities each week?

**Purpose:** Quantify resource drain and opportunity cost (feeds I1.1, I1.8)

**Listen for:**
- Hours per person per week
- Number of people involved
- Fully loaded cost (salary × time)
- Time breakdown: scheduling, prep, delivery, no-shows

**Probing Questions:**
- "How many demos do you schedule vs actually deliver?"
- "What percentage of scheduled demos are no-shows?"
- "How much prep time goes into each demo?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ "Not much time" (low cost = low willingness to pay)
- ⚠️ Can't quantify (doesn't track = doesn't care)
- ⚠️ "Under 2 hours/week total" (too small to solve)

---

#### Q5: Tell me about the last time a high-value prospect didn't show up for a scheduled demo. What happened?

**Purpose:** Understand business impact and emotional weight (feeds I1.1, I1.3)

**Listen for:**
- Specific recent example (within 30 days)
- Lost revenue or delayed deal
- Wasted preparation time
- Impact on sales rep morale

**Probing Questions:**
- "How often does this happen?"
- "What do you typically do when someone no-shows?"
- "Do you track no-show rates? What are they?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ "We don't really have no-shows" (skeptical—industry avg 40-60%)
- ⚠️ "It's not a big deal" (low emotional weight)
- ⚠️ Can't recall specific example

---

#### Q6: What have you tried to do to solve this problem?

**Purpose:** Identify workarounds and validate urgency (feeds I1.2 Workaround Rate)

**Listen for:**
- Active workarounds (proof they're trying to solve it)
- Multiple attempts (indicates persistence)
- Investment in partial solutions (time/money)
- Why workarounds are insufficient

**Probing Questions:**
- "How well did that work?"
- "Are you still using that approach?"
- "What about that solution isn't working?"
- "What else have you tried?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ "We haven't tried anything" (not urgent enough)
- ⚠️ "Our current solution works fine" (no need to switch)
- ⚠️ Gave up after one attempt (low motivation)

---

#### Q7: If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about your demo process, what would it be?

**Purpose:** Identify highest-priority pain point (feeds I1.1, I1.6)

**Listen for:**
- Specific, concrete improvement
- Alignment with your solution's core value prop
- Business outcome focus (not feature request)

**Probing Questions:**
- "Why is that the most important thing?"
- "What would that unlock for your business?"
- "What else would you fix after that?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Request unrelated to your solution (misalignment)
- ⚠️ "Everything is fine" (no pain)
- ⚠️ Minor cosmetic improvements (low value)

---

#### Q8: What does this problem cost your business—in revenue, time, or lost opportunities?

**Purpose:** Quantify business impact for ROI calculation (feeds I1.8, I1.9)

**Listen for:**
- Dollar amounts (lost deals, wasted time)
- Opportunity cost (what could they do instead)
- Strategic impact (market timing, competitor losses)

**Probing Questions:**
- "How many deals do you lose due to demo friction?"
- "What's the average deal size?"
- "How much are you spending on headcount to handle demos?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Can't quantify impact (doesn't track = doesn't matter)
- ⚠️ "Minimal cost" (low WTP)
- ⚠️ "I'm not sure" without trying to estimate

---

### Section 2: Solution Exploration (20 minutes)
**Goal:** Assess solution resonance, competitive displacement, and outcome alignment

---

**Transition:**
"Thanks for sharing all that. Based on what you've told me, let me describe an approach some companies are exploring and get your reaction..."

**Solution Description (keep neutral, don't pitch):**
"Imagine a system where prospects could get an instant, personalized product demo 24/7—no waiting for a sales rep. It would use AI to have natural conversations, answer questions, and adapt the demo based on what the prospect cares about. It would integrate with your CRM to capture all engagement data, and handle multiple languages.

This isn't about replacing your sales team—it's about giving prospects an on-demand option and freeing up your reps for high-value conversations with qualified leads who are further along."

---

#### Q9: What's your initial reaction to that concept?

**Purpose:** Gauge genuine interest vs politeness (feeds I1.4 Solution Resonance)

**Listen for:**
- Immediate enthusiasm ("that would be amazing")
- Skepticism or concerns (good—means they're thinking critically)
- Leaning forward vs leaning back (body language)
- Specific questions about implementation

**Probing Questions:**
- "What specifically appeals to you?"
- "What concerns come to mind?"
- "How would this fit into your current process?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Vague positivity ("that's interesting...") without specifics
- ⚠️ Immediate objections without exploring benefits
- ⚠️ "My team would never use that" (end user rejection)

---

#### Q10: Which part of that resonates most with your situation?

**Purpose:** Identify highest-value feature/outcome (feeds I1.6 Outcome Alignment)

**Listen for:**
- Instant availability (reduce wait time)
- Personalization (better lead experience)
- Sales rep time savings (efficiency)
- CRM integration (data capture)
- Multilingual support (market expansion)

**Probing Questions:**
- "Why is that piece so important to you?"
- "How would that change things for your team?"
- "What else matters to you in a solution like this?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Focuses on features you don't plan to build
- ⚠️ "None of it really fits" (misalignment)
- ⚠️ Can't articulate specific value

---

#### Q11: How does this compare to what you're doing today?

**Purpose:** Understand competitive alternatives (feeds I1.5 Competitive Displacement)

**Listen for:**
- Current alternatives (tools, manual process, workarounds)
- Gaps in current solutions
- Why they haven't fully solved it yet

**Probing Questions:**
- "What tools are you currently using for demos?"
- "What do those tools not do that this would?"
- "Why haven't existing solutions solved this?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ "This is exactly what we have" (no differentiation)
- ⚠️ Happy with current solution (no switching motivation)
- ⚠️ "We tried something like this and it didn't work"

---

#### Q12: What would need to be true for you to consider switching from your current approach?

**Purpose:** Identify switching barriers and displacement criteria (feeds I1.5)

**Listen for:**
- ROI requirements (2x, 5x, 10x improvement)
- Change management concerns (team adoption)
- Integration requirements (must work with Salesforce)
- Risk tolerance (need proof points, case studies)

**Probing Questions:**
- "What would make it a no-brainer to switch?"
- "What risks worry you about changing your process?"
- "How have you evaluated similar tools in the past?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Unrealistic requirements ("must do everything")
- ⚠️ High switching costs with no clear path
- ⚠️ "We'd never switch from [current vendor]"

---

#### Q13: What concerns or objections come to mind?

**Purpose:** Surface objections early (feeds I1.4, I1.5)

**Listen for:**
- Quality concerns ("can AI really do this?")
- Integration complexity
- Lead experience ("will prospects accept AI?")
- Control/customization needs

**Probing Questions:**
- "What would need to happen to address that concern?"
- "Have you seen other AI tools work well in sales?"
- "What would you need to see to believe this could work?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Fundamental philosophical objections ("AI can never replace humans")
- ⚠️ No trust in category/technology
- ⚠️ Multiple unresolvable objections

---

#### Q14: How would this impact your team's workflow?

**Purpose:** Assess implementation fit and change management (feeds I1.6)

**Listen for:**
- Positive impact on sales rep time
- Lead qualification improvement
- Workflow integration feasibility
- Training and adoption requirements

**Probing Questions:**
- "Who on your team would use this?"
- "How would this change what your SDRs/AEs do daily?"
- "What would need to change in your process to adopt this?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Requires massive process overhaul
- ⚠️ "This would create more work" (negative impact)
- ⚠️ No clear owner on the team

---

#### Q15: What outcomes would you measure to determine if this is successful?

**Purpose:** Validate outcome alignment and success criteria (feeds I1.6)

**Listen for:**
- Metrics they already track (demo show rate, conversion rate)
- Business outcomes (pipeline, revenue, rep productivity)
- Alignment with your solution's core value metrics

**Probing Questions:**
- "What would success look like in 3 months? 6 months?"
- "What metrics does your CEO care about for demos?"
- "How do you currently measure demo performance?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Metrics you can't influence (unrelated to solution)
- ⚠️ Can't define success ("I'm not sure what I'd measure")
- ⚠️ Success criteria impossible to achieve

---

#### Q16: If this worked as described, how much time would it save your team per week?

**Purpose:** Quantify value and ROI (feeds I1.6, I1.8, I1.9)

**Listen for:**
- Hours saved per person per week
- Number of people impacted
- What they'd do with saved time (higher-value activities)
- Willingness to pay based on time savings

**Probing Questions:**
- "What could your team do with that extra time?"
- "How many more deals could you close with that capacity?"
- "What's the loaded cost of that time today?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Minimal time savings (<5 hours/week total)
- ⚠️ "We'd just reduce headcount" (doesn't create value)
- ⚠️ Can't articulate how time would be redeployed

---

### Section 3: Willingness to Pay (20 minutes)
**Goal:** Understand budget, current spend, and pricing sensitivity

---

**Transition:**
"Thanks for that feedback. Now I want to understand the economics side—not pitching pricing, just trying to learn what makes sense for companies like yours..."

---

#### Q17: Do you currently have a budget allocated for sales enablement or demo tools?

**Purpose:** Assess budget existence and planning (feeds I1.7 Budget Existence)

**Listen for:**
- "Yes" with specific amount (ideal)
- Budget planning cycle (annual, quarterly)
- Budget owner (RevOps, Sales, Marketing)
- Flexibility to reallocate or expand budget

**Probing Questions:**
- "What's in that budget currently?"
- "When do you typically make decisions on new tools?"
- "Who controls that budget?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ "No budget" with no path to create one
- ⚠️ Budget is fully allocated with no flexibility
- ⚠️ "I'm not involved in budget decisions"

---

#### Q18: What do you currently spend on demo-related tools and processes?

**Purpose:** Establish current spend baseline (feeds I1.8 Current Spend)

**Listen for:**
- Tool subscriptions (Calendly, DemoStack, Consensus, etc.)
- Headcount cost (FTEs dedicated to demos)
- Indirect costs (CRM licenses, meeting tools)
- Total monthly/annual spend

**Probing Questions:**
- "What tools are you paying for today?"
- "How many people are dedicated to demo delivery?"
- "What's their fully loaded cost (salary + benefits)?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Current spend under $500/month (too small to anchor pricing)
- ⚠️ "We don't spend anything" (have to create budget)
- ⚠️ Can't estimate spend (not financially aware)

---

#### Q19: If you could reduce demo no-shows by 50% and save your team 10 hours per week, what would that be worth?

**Purpose:** Anchor value and establish ROI thinking (feeds I1.9 WTP)

**Listen for:**
- Dollar value calculation
- ROI multiple (value vs cost tolerance)
- Comparison to other tools they pay for

**Probing Questions:**
- "How much does it cost you when a demo no-shows?"
- "What's 10 hours of your team's time worth?"
- "What would that do for your pipeline and revenue?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Can't articulate value ("I don't know")
- ⚠️ Value is very low relative to solution cost
- ⚠️ "That improvement wouldn't matter much"

---

#### Q20: What do you pay for similar sales tools today?

**Purpose:** Establish pricing comparables (feeds I1.8, I1.9)

**Listen for:**
- Per-user pricing models (e.g., $100/user/month)
- Platform pricing (e.g., $2K/month flat)
- What they consider "expensive" vs "reasonable"

**Probing Questions:**
- "What's the most you pay for a single sales tool?"
- "What's the least you pay for something critical?"
- "How do you think about pricing—per user, per demo, platform?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ All tools are <$50/month (very price-sensitive)
- ⚠️ "We'd never pay more than $X" where X is very low
- ⚠️ No reference points (doesn't buy tools)

---

### Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter (Q21-Q24)

**Introduction:**
"I'm going to ask you four pricing questions to understand what price range would make sense. There are no wrong answers—just looking for your gut reaction."

---

#### Q21: At what price would this solution be so expensive that you would not consider buying it?

**Purpose:** Identify ceiling price (feeds I1.9 WTP)

**Record:** $__________ (Too Expensive price)

**Listen for:**
- Quick answer (already has pricing expectation)
- Hesitation (no mental model for pricing)
- Explanation of why ("because that's more than...")

**Probing Questions:**
- "Why that number?"
- "What would you compare it to at that price?"

---

#### Q22: At what price would you consider this solution to be expensive, but still worth considering?

**Purpose:** Identify premium pricing threshold (feeds I1.9 WTP)

**Record:** $__________ (Expensive but Acceptable price)

**Listen for:**
- Should be lower than "too expensive" price
- Rationale based on value or comparison

**Probing Questions:**
- "What would make it worth it at that price?"
- "What would you need to see to justify paying that?"

---

#### Q23: At what price would you consider this solution to be a bargain—a great deal?

**Purpose:** Identify low-end price perception (feeds I1.9 WTP)

**Record:** $__________ (Great Deal price)

**Listen for:**
- Floor price for credibility
- "Too good to be true" threshold

**Probing Questions:**
- "Why would that feel like a bargain?"
- "Would you worry about quality at that price?"

---

#### Q24: At what price would this solution be so inexpensive that you'd question its quality?

**Purpose:** Identify price floor (feeds I1.9 WTP)

**Record:** $__________ (Too Cheap price)

**Listen for:**
- Should be lower than "bargain" price
- Quality concerns at low price

**Probing Questions:**
- "Why would that price make you skeptical?"
- "What's the cheapest tool you use that actually works?"

#### Q25: If this were available today at [median acceptable price from Q22], how likely would you be to start a trial or proof of concept?

**Purpose:** Assess purchase intent (feeds I1.10 Purchase Intent)

**Scale:** 1 (Very Unlikely) to 5 (Very Likely)

**Record:** _____ / 5

**Listen for:**
- 4-5 = strong intent (target: ≥50% of interviews)
- 3 = neutral (needs more convincing)
- 1-2 = low intent (either wrong ICP or solution misfit)

**Probing Questions (for 4-5):**
- "What would need to happen next?"
- "Who else would need to be involved in that decision?"
- "What's your typical buying process for new tools?"

**Probing Questions (for 1-3):**
- "What's holding you back?"
- "What would need to change for this to be a 5?"
- "Is this the wrong time, wrong solution, or wrong price?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ Score of 3 or below (weak intent)
- ⚠️ "I'd need to see it first" without commitment to trial
- ⚠️ "Maybe in 6-12 months" (not urgent)

#### Q26: What would your evaluation process look like?

**Purpose:** Understand buying cycle and decision-makers (validates I1.7, I1.10)

**Listen for:**
- Number of stakeholders involved
- Timeline (days, weeks, months)
- Approval process (trials, legal, procurement)
- Success criteria for pilot

**Probing Questions:**
- "Who else would need to sign off?"
- "How long do tool evaluations typically take?"
- "What would a pilot need to prove?"

**Red Flags:**
- ⚠️ 6+ month buying cycle (too long)
- ⚠️ 10+ stakeholders (enterprise sales complexity)
- ⚠️ "I'd need board approval" (out of budget authority)

#### Q27: Is there anything else about this problem or solution that I should have asked about?

**Purpose:** Catch blind spots and surface additional insights

**Listen for:**
- Concerns you didn't address
- Use cases you didn't consider
- Competitive intel or market insights

**Probing Questions:**
- "What did I miss?"
- "What would you want to know if you were doing this research?"

## The Mom Test Reminders

### Core Principles for Interviewers

**1. Talk About Their Life, Not Your Idea**
- ❌ Bad: "Would you use an AI demo platform?"
- ✅ Good: "Tell me about the last time a prospect no-showed a demo."

**2. Ask About Specifics in the Past, Not Opinions About the Future**
- ❌ Bad: "Would you pay $500/month for this?"
- ✅ Good: "What do you currently spend on demo tools?"

**3. Listen More Than You Talk**
- Target: 70% listening, 30% talking
- Use "Tell me more..." instead of leading questions
- Embrace awkward silence—let them fill it

**4. Don't Pitch—Present Neutrally**
- ❌ Bad: "This amazing AI solution will revolutionize your demos!"
- ✅ Good: "Some companies are exploring AI-led demos. What's your reaction?"

**5. Look for Evidence, Not Enthusiasm**
- Discount "That's a great idea!" (politeness)
- Value "Here's what I tried last week..." (action)
- Trust money/time spent (revealed preference)

**6. Dig Into Workarounds**
- If they have workarounds = problem is real
- If workarounds are expensive/painful = high willingness to pay
- If no workarounds = problem not urgent enough

**7. Watch for False Positives**
- Compliments without commitment = politeness
- "I would definitely..." = hypothetical (discount it)
- Excitement without budget/urgency = not a real buyer

**8. Red Flag Language**
- "Usually..." "Probably..." "Might..." = hypothetical
- "Everyone says..." = not their personal experience
- "I would..." = future hypothetical, not past action

**9. Green Flag Language**
- "Last week..." "Yesterday..." = recent and real
- "I spent $X on..." = revealed preference
- "I tried..." = active problem-solving
- Specific numbers, dates, names = concrete evidence

## Tips for First-Time Interviewers

**Before the interview:**
- Practice the script 2-3 times out loud
- Memorize key questions (don't read robotically)
- Prepare to take notes efficiently (or record with permission)

**During the interview:**
- Start with easy questions to build rapport
- Follow their energy—dig deeper when they get animated
- Don't be afraid to go off-script if they reveal something interesting
- Take notes on exact phrases (great for landing page copy later)

**After the interview:**
- Immediately fill out tracking template (while fresh)
- Note your gut feeling about purchase intent
- Identify 2-3 key insights to carry forward
- Send gift card within 24 hours

**Common mistakes:**
- Talking too much (you should talk 30% max)
- Defending your idea when they critique it (just listen!)
- Skipping the hard questions (budget, purchase intent)
- Not asking for referrals at the end
