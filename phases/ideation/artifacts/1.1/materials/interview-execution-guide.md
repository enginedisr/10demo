# Interview Execution Guide - Recipe 1.1
## Problem Validation Interviews for 10Demo

---

## Overview

### Venture Context

**Problem**: VPs of Sales at B2B SaaS companies face a critical pipeline problem where prospects wait 24-120 hours for product demos and receive generic, one-size-fits-all presentations. This leads to 40-60% demo no-shows and massive pipeline leakage, with an inability to scale demos without adding expensive headcount.

**Solution**: 10Demo is an AI agent that instantly joins live video calls with prospects the moment they request a demo—no waiting, no scheduling. The AI delivers personalized, context-aware product demonstrations in any language, integrates with CRM systems, and operates 24/7.

**Target Market**: B2B SaaS companies with high demo volume and pipeline leakage issues

### Interview Goals

This interview guide will help you conduct 10-15 customer discovery interviews to validate:

1. **Problem Severity**: Do VPs of Sales actually experience this demo scheduling and no-show problem?
2. **Problem Frequency**: How often does this problem occur and impact their business?
3. **Current Solutions**: What workarounds and alternatives are they using today?
4. **Solution Resonance**: Does the AI demo agent concept resonate with their needs?
5. **Willingness to Pay**: What budget exists and what would they pay for this solution?

### Success Criteria (Recipe 1.1 KPIs)

- **I1.1 Problem Confirmation**: ≥70% acknowledge the problem
- **I1.2 Pain Intensity**: Average ≥7/10 pain score
- **I1.3 Problem Recency**: ≥60% experienced problem in last 30 days
- **I1.4 Workaround Existence**: ≥50% have active workarounds (indicates real pain)
- **I1.5 Solution Interest**: ≥70% express genuine interest
- **I1.6 Competitive Displacement**: ≥60% willing to switch from current solution
- **I1.7 Outcome Alignment**: ≥70% see solution addressing their desired outcomes
- **I1.8 Budget Existence**: ≥50% have budget for this category
- **I1.9 Current Spend**: Average spend ≥$50K/year on demo-related costs
- **I1.10 Purchase Intent**: Average ≥3.5/5 on purchase intent scale

## Target Personas

### Persona 1: VP of Sales at High-Growth B2B SaaS (Primary Buyer)

**Segment**: Mid-Market to Enterprise

**Role**:
- Job Title: VP of Sales, Head of Sales, CRO
- Department: Sales Leadership
- Seniority: Executive level (C-level or direct report to CEO/CRO)
- Responsibilities: Pipeline management, revenue targets, sales team scaling

**Company Profile**:
- Size: 50-500 employees
- Revenue: $10M-$100M ARR
- Sales Team: 10-50+ sales reps
- Industry: B2B SaaS (any vertical)
- Demo Volume: 50-500+ demos per month
- Tech Stack: Salesforce/HubSpot, Gong/Chorus, Calendly/Chili Piper

**Why Interview**:
- Primary economic buyer - owns the budget
- Directly accountable for pipeline conversion and demo no-show rates
- Feels the pain of scaling demo capacity
- Makes or heavily influences purchasing decisions for sales tools

**Interview Focus**:
- Problem severity: demo no-shows, scheduling delays, pipeline leakage
- Current costs: AE/SDR time spent on demos, opportunity cost of delays
- Workarounds: hiring more reps, scheduling tools, demo automation attempts
- Willingness to Pay: budget for sales productivity tools
- Purchase authority and procurement process

**Recommended Count**: 5-6 interviews (40-50% of total)

- Training challenges: onboarding new reps on product demos
- Process inefficiencies: demo scheduling, follow-up, CRM integration
- Metrics tracked: demo-to-opportunity conversion, no-show rates

**Recommended Count**: 3-4 interviews (25-30% of total)

- No-show impact on quota and productivity

## Recruitment Strategy

### Target: 10-15 Total Interviews

**Distribution**:
- 5-6 VPs of Sales (40-50%)
- 3-4 Sales Enablement Leaders (25-30%)
- 2-3 Account Executives (15-20%)
- 1-2 Founders/CEOs (10-15%)

**Timeline**: 3-4 weeks to complete all interviews

### Recruitment Channels

#### 1. LinkedIn Outreach (Primary Channel)

**Target Profiles**:
- VP of Sales at B2B SaaS companies with 50-500 employees
- Sales Enablement leaders at high-growth SaaS companies
- Account Executives at companies with high demo volume

**Search Filters**:
- Job Title: "VP of Sales", "Head of Sales", "CRO", "Director Sales Enablement"
- Industry: "Computer Software", "SaaS", "Information Technology"
- Company Size: 50-500 employees
- Location: US, Canada, UK (English-speaking for easier interviews)

**Outreach Cadence**:
- Day 1: Send connection request with note
- Day 3: If accepted, send interview request message
- Day 7: Follow-up if no response
- Day 10: Final follow-up

#### 2. Existing Network (Warmest Channel)

**Tap into**:
- Your personal LinkedIn connections in SaaS sales
- Previous colleagues from SaaS companies
- Investors, advisors, or mentors who can make introductions
- SaaS founder communities you're part of

**Approach**:
- Direct message or email with context
- "Would love 25 minutes of your time to learn about [problem]"
- Emphasize learning, not selling

#### 3. SaaS Communities & Slack Groups

**Target Communities**:
- SaaStr Community
- Pavilion (Revenue Collective)
- Sales Enablement Society
- SaaS Growth Hacks groups
- Relevant subreddits (r/sales, r/SaaS, r/startups)

**Approach**:
- Post asking for interview volunteers (if allowed)
- DM members whose profiles fit persona
- Offer to share anonymized findings as value exchange

#### 4. Conferences & Events (If Timing Aligns)

**Target Events**:
- SaaStr Annual/Regional
- Sales Enablement Society events
- Local SaaS meetups
- Virtual sales conferences

**Approach**:
- Schedule interviews at the event or post-event
- Use event as conversation starter in outreach

### Incentives & Value Exchange

**What to Offer**:

1. **Share Findings** (Most Valuable for Executives)
   - "I'll send you a summary of what I'm learning from other sales leaders"
   - Anonymized insights on demo no-show rates, best practices, benchmarks

2. **Gift Card** (For Busy Executives)
   - $50-100 Amazon/Starbucks gift card for their time
   - Mention in initial outreach: "Happy to send a $50 gift card as a thank you"

3. **Early Access** (For Those Who Express Interest)
   - "If we build this, you'll be first to try it"
   - Beta access with discounted pricing
   - Note: Only mention if they express genuine interest organically

4. **Networking/Introduction** (Peer Value)
   - Connect them with other sales leaders doing interesting things
   - Share relevant content or tools you've come across

**What NOT to Do**:
- Don't pitch your solution in outreach
- Don't promise features or pricing you're unsure about
- Don't overpromise value exchange you can't deliver

### Scheduling Logistics

**Tools**:
- **Calendly** or **Cal.com**: Share booking link for easy scheduling
- **Zoom** or **Google Meet**: For video calls (always use video if possible)
- **Notion** or **Airtable**: Track interview status and responses

**Timing**:
- Aim for 30-minute slots (allows 25 mins interview + 5 min buffer)
- Offer morning (9-10am) or late afternoon (4-5pm) slots
- Avoid Mondays (busy) and Fridays (low show rate)

**Confirmation**:
- Send calendar invite immediately after they agree
- Include Zoom link, brief context, and your email for questions
- Send reminder 24 hours before interview

## Interview Script

- [ ] Review interviewee's LinkedIn profile and company

- [ ] Review this interview script
- [ ] Open tracking template to record responses
- [ ] Test Zoom/recording setup (ask permission to record)
- [ ] Have notebook ready for additional notes

**Opening (2 minutes)**:
```
"Hi [Name], thanks so much for taking the time today.

Just to set context: I'm researching challenges that B2B SaaS companies face
around product demos - specifically scheduling, no-shows, and demo quality.

I'm not here to pitch anything - I genuinely want to learn from your experience.
Everything you share will be kept confidential, and I'll send you a summary of
what I'm learning from other sales leaders.

Would you mind if I record this call? It's just for my notes - I won't share
the recording with anyone.

Sound good? Great - let's dive in."
```

### Section 1: Problem Discovery (20 minutes)
**Focus**: Past behavior, specific examples, pain intensity

---

#### Q1: Walk me through your demo process today, from the moment a prospect requests a demo to when it actually happens.

**Purpose**: Understand current workflow and identify pain points organically (feeds I1.1, I1.3)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Steps involved: lead capture → scheduling → confirmation → demo execution
- People involved: SDRs, AEs, coordinators
- Tools used: Calendly, Chili Piper, CRM
- Time delays between request and demo
- Friction points they mention naturally

**Probing Questions**:
- "How long does it typically take from demo request to scheduled demo?"
- "Who's involved in getting a demo scheduled?"
- "What happens if the prospect is in a different time zone?"

**Red Flags**:
- "Our process works perfectly" (unlikely - probe deeper)
- Too vague - need specific examples
- Defensive tone about current process

---

#### Q2: Tell me about the last time a prospect requested a demo. What happened?

**Purpose**: Get specific, recent example to validate problem recency (feeds I1.3)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Specific story with details (when, who, what happened)
- Timeline from request to demo
- Any issues that came up (no-show, reschedule, delay)
- Emotional response to the situation

**Probing Questions**:
- "When exactly was this?" (validate recency)
- "How did the prospect reach out - form fill, sales call, email?"
- "Did the demo happen as scheduled, or were there any changes?"
- "How did you feel about how that went?"

**Red Flags**:
- Can't remember a recent example (problem may not be frequent)
- Says "usually" or "typically" instead of specific story
- Hypothetical language: "what we would do is..."

---

#### Q3: In the last month, about how many demos did your team schedule? And how many of those actually happened?

**Purpose**: Quantify demo volume and no-show rate (feeds I1.1, I1.3, I1.9)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Specific numbers (even rough estimates)
- No-show rate calculation (if they track it)
- Whether they know these metrics off the top of their head (indicates pain)
- Surprise or frustration when calculating the gap

**Probing Questions**:
- "Is that higher or lower than usual?"
- "Do you track demo no-show rates formally?"
- "What's your target no-show rate vs. actual?"
- "How does that no-show rate impact your pipeline?"

**Red Flags**:
- No idea of the numbers (may not be tracking, or not a real pain point)
- Very low no-show rate (<10%) - problem may not exist
- Doesn't seem bothered by high no-show rate

---

#### Q4: On a scale of 1-10, how painful is the demo scheduling and no-show problem for you? Why that number?

**Purpose**: Quantify pain intensity (feeds I1.2)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Numerical score (7+ is strong validation)
- Reasoning behind the score
- Specific consequences they mention (lost deals, wasted time, missed quota)
- Emotional language: "frustrated", "losing sleep", "critical issue"

**Probing Questions**:
- "What would make it a 10?"
- "How much pipeline are you losing due to this?"
- "How does this affect your team's ability to hit quota?"
- "If you could wave a magic wand and fix one thing about demos, what would it be?"

**Red Flags**:
- Score below 5 (problem isn't severe enough)
- Generic reasoning ("it's annoying but manageable")
- Quickly moves on - doesn't elaborate on pain

---

#### Q5: What have you tried to solve this demo scheduling or no-show problem?

**Purpose**: Understand current workarounds and validate problem severity (feeds I1.4)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Active workarounds indicate real pain
- Money/time already invested in solutions
- What worked and what didn't
- Ongoing vs. abandoned attempts

**Probing Questions**:
- "How long have you been using [workaround]?"
- "How much does [workaround] cost you?"
- "What do you like about it? What's missing?"
- "If you could keep one thing about your current setup, what would it be?"

**Red Flags**:
- "We haven't tried anything" (maybe not a real problem)
- "We're happy with our current solution" (hard to displace)
- Tried many solutions and nothing worked (skeptical buyer)

---

#### Q6: Before you implemented [workaround from Q5], what were you doing? How was that different?

**Purpose**: Understand pain progression and investment in solutions (feeds I1.4, I1.9)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Evolution of the problem over time
- Previous attempts and why they changed
- Growing pain as company scaled
- Budget allocated to solving this

**Probing Questions**:
- "What triggered you to change approaches?"
- "How much time/money were you spending before?"
- "What was the breaking point that made you look for a solution?"

**Red Flags**:
- "We've always done it this way" (no urgency)
- Can't remember what they did before (not a pressing issue)

---

#### Q7: Tell me about a time when a high-value prospect didn't show up for a scheduled demo. What happened to that deal?

**Purpose**: Understand consequences of the problem (feeds I1.1, I1.2)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Specific story (not hypothetical)
- Deal value and stage
- Outcome: Did they reschedule? Did deal die?
- Emotional impact on rep/team
- Opportunity cost

**Probing Questions**:
- "How much was that deal worth?"
- "How many times did you try to reschedule?"
- "Where is that deal now?"
- "How often does this happen?"

**Red Flags**:
- Can't think of a specific example
- "It all worked out fine" (low consequence)
- Blames prospect, not the process

---

#### Q8: If you could eliminate demo scheduling delays and no-shows completely, how would that impact your business?

**Purpose**: Understand desired outcomes and value (feeds I1.7)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Specific business metrics: pipeline, conversion rate, revenue
- Quantified impact if possible
- How this ties to their goals/OKRs
- Team efficiency gains

**Probing Questions**:
- "How much more pipeline could you generate?"
- "How would this affect your team's time allocation?"
- "What would you do with the time saved?"
- "How much revenue is on the table if you could solve this?"

**Red Flags**:
- Vague, generic answer ("it would be nice")
- Can't articulate business impact
- Impact seems small relative to effort

### Section 2: Solution Exploration (20 minutes)
**Focus**: Solution resonance, competitive displacement, outcome alignment

**IMPORTANT**: Present the solution concept neutrally. Don't pitch. Listen for genuine resonance vs. politeness.

---

#### Q9: Now that I understand your situation, let me share what we're exploring and get your reaction.

**[Present Solution Concept - 60 seconds max]**

```
"We're exploring an AI agent that instantly joins video calls with prospects
the moment they request a demo - no scheduling, no waiting.

The AI delivers a personalized product demo based on the prospect's company,
role, and use case. It pulls data from your CRM and adapts the demo in real-time.
It works 24/7, in any language, and can handle unlimited demos simultaneously.

After the demo, it hands off qualified leads to your sales team with a full
summary of what was discussed and buyer intent signals.

That's the concept we're exploring. What's your immediate reaction?"
```

**Purpose**: Gauge initial solution interest (feeds I1.5)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Genuine excitement vs. polite interest
- Specific aspects that resonate
- Immediate concerns or objections
- Questions about how it works (indicates interest)

**Probing Questions**:
- "What aspects of this resonate with you?"
- "What concerns come to mind immediately?"
- "How would this fit into your current workflow?"

**Red Flags**:
- Flat "that's interesting" with no follow-up
- Immediately says "our customers would never accept AI"
- Seems confused about the concept

---

#### Q10: Thinking about what you just heard, what specific problem would this solve for you?

**Purpose**: Validate they understand the value proposition (feeds I1.7)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Do they articulate the core problems we solve?
- Do they connect it to their specific pain points from Section 1?
- Do they add problems we didn't mention?

**Probing Questions**:
- "Which problem is most valuable to solve - the scheduling delay or the no-shows?"
- "Are there other problems this might solve that I didn't mention?"

**Red Flags**:
- Misunderstands what the solution does
- Can't connect it to their pain points
- Focuses on features, not problems solved

---

#### Q11: You mentioned you're currently using [workaround from Q5]. How does this concept compare to what you're doing today?

**Purpose**: Assess competitive displacement potential (feeds I1.6)

**What You're Listening For**:
- See clear advantages over current solution
- Willing to consider switching
- What they'd gain and what they'd lose
- Switching costs/friction they mention

**Probing Questions**:
- "What would this do better than [current solution]?"
- "What would you lose if you switched?"
- "How hard would it be to switch from [current solution] to this?"
- "What would need to be true for you to consider switching?"

**Red Flags**:
- "Our current solution is better"
- High switching costs with low perceived benefit
- Lock-in with current vendor (long contract, integration debt)

---

#### Q12: If this worked exactly as I described, which of your current demos would you want the AI to handle first?

**Purpose**: Understand use case prioritization and adoption path (feeds I1.7)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Specific demo types/segments they'd start with
- Low-stakes vs. high-value demos
- Use cases where AI has clear advantage
- Potential adoption pathway

**Probing Questions**:
- "Why start with those demos?"
- "What demos would you definitely NOT want AI to handle?"
- "How would you roll this out to your team?"

**Red Flags**:
- "None - we need humans for all demos"
- Only willing to use for very low-value demos (limits market size)
- Can't articulate a clear use case

---

#### Q13: What concerns would you have about using an AI agent to run demos for you?

**Purpose**: Surface objections early (feeds I1.5, I1.6)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Quality concerns: "Can AI really demo our complex product?"
- Customer acceptance: "Will prospects be turned off by AI?"
- Control/trust: "What if it says something wrong?"
- Differentiation: "Demos are how we build relationships"

**Probing Questions**:
- "What would need to be true for you to trust the AI with demos?"
- "Have you used AI for customer-facing interactions before? How did it go?"
- "If we could address [concern], would you be interested?"

**Red Flags**:
- Fundamental objection you can't overcome ("never using AI for customers")
- Multiple deal-breaker concerns
- Philosophical opposition to automation in sales

---

#### Q14: Let's say you implemented this and it worked. Six months from now, what would success look like?

**Purpose**: Validate outcome alignment with their goals (feeds I1.7)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Specific metrics they'd measure
- How it ties to their OKRs/goals
- Team impact and workflow changes
- Business outcomes beyond just "more demos"

**Probing Questions**:
- "What metrics would you track?"
- "How would your team's day-to-day work change?"
- "How would this impact your quarterly revenue targets?"

**Red Flags**:
- Can't articulate success metrics
- Success criteria are vague ("things would be better")
- Success scenario is unrealistic

---

#### Q15: Who else at your company would need to be involved in evaluating or approving something like this?

**Purpose**: Understand buying process and decision-makers (feeds I1.8)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Decision-making authority: are they the buyer?
- Other stakeholders: CRO, CFO, RevOps, IT/Security
- Approval process and timeline
- Budget ownership

**Probing Questions**:
- "Walk me through how you typically evaluate and buy sales tools."
- "Who owns the budget for something like this?"
- "What's the typical timeline from initial interest to purchase?"
- "What would make this a 'no-brainer' approval vs. a tough sell internally?"

**Red Flags**:
- They have no buying authority and limited influence
- Complex, lengthy approval process (6+ month sales cycle)
- Multiple veto holders with different priorities

---

#### Q16: On a scale of 1-5, where 1 is 'not interested' and 5 is 'this solves a critical problem and I'd want to try it immediately', where would you be?

**Purpose**: Quantify solution interest level (feeds I1.5)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Numerical score (3.5+ is strong validation)
- Reasoning behind the score
- Conditions that would move the score up
- Genuine enthusiasm vs. polite interest

**Probing Questions**:
- "What would move you from a [X] to a [X+1]?"
- "What's holding you back from being a 5?"
- "If this was available today, what would be your next step?"

**Red Flags**:
- Score of 2 or below
- Can't articulate what would increase their interest
- Says "maybe someday" instead of "when can I try it"

---

### Section 3: Willingness to Pay (20 minutes)
**Focus**: Budget, current spend, pricing sensitivity, purchase intent

**IMPORTANT**: This is the hardest section. People avoid talking about money. Be direct but not aggressive. Frame around value and existing spend.

---

#### Q17: Let's talk about budget and cost. Is solving this demo scheduling problem something you'd allocate budget to?

**Purpose**: Validate budget existence (feeds I1.8)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Clear "yes" or "we already have budget for this category"
- Budget owner and approval process
- Budget cycle timing (annual, quarterly)
- Existing budget allocated to related tools

**Probing Questions**:
- "Do you have budget for sales productivity tools this year?"
- "Who controls that budget?"
- "When do you typically make these types of purchases?"

**Red Flags**:
- "No budget" or "we'd have to find money"
- Budget frozen or heavily constrained
- Wrong budget cycle timing (just completed annual planning)

---

#### Q18: What do you currently spend on tools and people related to running demos?

**Purpose**: Establish baseline current spend (feeds I1.9)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Direct costs: Calendly, Chili Piper, demo automation tools
- People costs: SDR/AE time, dedicated demo engineers, coordinators
- Indirect costs: CRM, sales enablement platforms, video tools
- Total spend (even rough estimate)

**Probing Questions**:
- "What tools do you use for demo scheduling, and what do they cost?"
- "How much of your AE team's time goes to demos?"
- "Do you have dedicated demo engineers or SEs? What's that cost?"
- "If you had to estimate total annual cost of your demo function, what would it be?"

**Red Flags**:
- Can't estimate costs (may not have authority or visibility)
- Very low spend (<$10K/year) - limits WTP for our solution
- "We don't really spend anything" (contradicts having the problem)

---

#### Q19: If you could reduce demo no-shows by 40-60% and eliminate scheduling delays, what would that be worth to your business?

**Purpose**: Establish value perception before discussing price (feeds I1.9, I1.7)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Dollar value or business impact quantification
- Pipeline impact, revenue impact, efficiency gains
- How they calculate ROI
- Whether value significantly exceeds likely price

**Probing Questions**:
- "How much pipeline do you lose to demo no-shows today?"
- "What's the value of an extra [X] qualified demos per month?"
- "How much is an AE's time worth when reallocated from demos?"

**Red Flags**:
- Can't quantify value ("it would be nice but not sure what it's worth")
- Value is very small relative to likely price point
- Doesn't see clear ROI

---

#### Q20: Just to calibrate expectations - we haven't set pricing yet, but solutions in this category range from $500-$2000 per month for teams your size. Does that range feel reasonable, too expensive, or actually pretty affordable?

**Purpose**: Anchor on ballpark pricing and gauge price sensitivity (feeds I1.10)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Initial price reaction (sticker shock vs. "that's reasonable")
- How they contextualize it vs. current spend or value
- Whether they start doing ROI math
- Budget constraints that surface

**Probing Questions**:
- "How does that compare to what you're spending today on demo tools?"
- "What would justify that price point for you?"
- "Where in that range would you expect this to be priced?"

**Red Flags**:
- Immediate sticker shock ("that's way too expensive")
- "We'd never spend more than $[much lower amount]"
- Shuts down conversation after hearing price range

### Van Westendorp Price Sensitivity Meter (Q21-Q24)

**Instructions**: Ask all four questions. Don't explain the methodology - just ask directly.

---

#### Q21: At what price would you consider this product to be so expensive that you would not consider buying it? (Too Expensive)

**Purpose**: Establish price ceiling (feeds I1.10)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Specific dollar amount per month or per year
- Whether they anchor on value or budget constraints
- How they justify the number

**Probing Questions**:
- "What makes that the absolute maximum?"
- "Is that per month or per year?"

---

#### Q22: At what price would you consider this product to be priced so low that you'd feel the quality couldn't be very good? (Too Cheap)

**Purpose**: Establish price floor and quality expectations (feeds I1.10)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Lower bound of acceptable pricing
- Quality expectations tied to price
- Whether they see this as a premium or commodity solution

**Probing Questions**:
- "What would make you skeptical at that price point?"

---

#### Q23: At what price would you consider this product to be starting to get expensive, but you'd still consider it? (Getting Expensive)

**Purpose**: Upper bound of comfortable pricing (feeds I1.10)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Where they start to hesitate
- Price point that requires stronger justification
- Budget vs. value trade-off point

---

#### Q24: At what price would you consider this product to be a bargain—a great buy for the value? (Great Value)

**Purpose**: Optimal price point from customer perspective (feeds I1.10)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Sweet spot pricing
- Where value clearly exceeds cost
- Budget-friendly price point

---

#### Q25: How does your company typically pay for software like this - monthly subscription, annual contract, usage-based?

**Purpose**: Understand pricing model preferences (feeds I1.8, I1.10)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Preferred payment structure
- Contract length expectations
- Procurement constraints (must be annual, etc.)
- Usage-based vs. flat fee preference

**Probing Questions**:
- "What do you prefer and why?"
- "Does your company have a policy on contract lengths?"
- "Would you prefer pricing per demo, per user, or flat fee?"

**Red Flags**:
- Rigid constraints that don't match your business model
- "We only do multi-year contracts" (long sales cycle)

---

#### Q26: If this was available in 60 days, on a scale of 1-5, how likely would you be to purchase it?

**Purpose**: Assess purchase intent with realistic timeline (feeds I1.10)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Numerical score (4+ is strong validation)
- Conditions for purchase
- Timeline and urgency
- Commitment level

**Probing Questions**:
- "What would need to happen in those 60 days for you to buy?"
- "What would make you a 5?"
- "What could drop you to a 1?"
- "If we gave you early beta access at 50% off, would you be interested?"

**Red Flags**:
- Score of 2 or below
- "Let me see how competitors do this first"
- "Check back with me next year"

---

#### Q27: If you were me, who else should I talk to that faces similar demo challenges?

**Purpose**: Get referrals and validate persona (feeds recruitment pipeline)

**What You're Listening For**:
- Specific names and companies
- Other personas who face this problem
- Warm introduction vs. cold outreach
- Signal of how helpful they want to be

**Probing Questions**:
- "Would you be comfortable making an introduction?"
- "What should I know about them before reaching out?"

## The Mom Test Principles - Quick Reference

Keep these principles in mind throughout every interview:

### 1. Talk About Their Life, Not Your Idea
- ❌ Bad: "Would you use a product that instantly schedules demos?"
- ✅ Good: "Tell me about the last time a prospect requested a demo."

### 2. Ask About Specifics in the Past, Not Generics or the Future
- ❌ Bad: "How often do demos get no-shows?"
- ✅ Good: "In the last month, how many demos did you schedule vs. how many happened?"

### 3. Talk Less, Listen More
- Aim for 70% listening, 30% talking
- Use silence - let them fill it
- When they say something interesting, say "Tell me more about that"

### 4. Don't Pitch Your Solution
- Present the concept neutrally
- Let them tell you if it resonates
- Listen for genuine excitement vs. politeness

### 5. Ask for Commitments, Not Compliments
- ❌ Bad: "Do you think this is a good idea?"
- ✅ Good: "If this existed today, would you allocate budget to buy it?"

### 6. Dig Into Emotions
- When they mention frustration, dig deeper: "Tell me more about that"
- Look for passion in their voice - that's where real pain lives
- Flat affect = low pain or politeness

### 7. Ask "Why?" to Get to Root Causes
- They mention a workaround? Ask "Why did you start doing that?"
- They want a feature? Ask "Why is that important to you?"
- Peel the onion until you hit bedrock

### 8. Watch for Red Flags
- Generic answers ("we'd probably use it")
- Hypothetical language ("we would be interested")
- Compliments without commitments ("that's a great idea!")
- Can't remember specific examples

### 9. Get Specifics on Numbers
- Don't accept "a lot" - ask "about how many?"
- Demo volume, no-show rate, current spend, budget
- Even rough estimates are valuable

### 10. End with Referrals
- If the interview went well, ask for 2-3 referrals
- This validates that you're on to something real

## After You've Completed All Interviews

Once you've conducted 10-15 interviews, you'll be ready for the next step: **Interview Analysis** (Recipe 1.1 Analyzer Agent).

The analyzer will:
- Calculate all 10 KPIs (I1.1 - I1.10)
- Determine if you've achieved "Problem Validated" status
- Identify which personas have strongest product-market fit
- Surface key insights and patterns across interviews
- Recommend next steps based on validation results

**You'll Need**:
- Completed tracking template with all interview data
- Any additional notes or recordings from interviews
- Persona distribution summary

**Success Looks Like**:
- ≥70% problem confirmation (I1.1)
- ≥7/10 average pain score (I1.2)
- ≥60% problem recency (I1.3)
- ≥50% have workarounds (I1.4)
- ≥70% solution interest (I1.5)
- ≥60% competitive displacement (I1.6)
- ≥70% outcome alignment (I1.7)
- ≥50% budget existence (I1.8)
- ≥$50K average current spend (I1.9)
- ≥3.5/5 average purchase intent (I1.10)

If you hit these thresholds, you've validated the problem and are ready to move forward. If not, the analyzer will help you understand which areas need work and whether to pivot, persevere, or iterate on your ICP.
